Research: NIT Jamshedpur Gym Crowd Management
1. Problem Statement
- Problem to investigate: The NIT Jamshedpur gym has limited space (approximately 10 ft × 10 ft, based on my current understanding) and is shared by students from B.Tech, M.Tech, MCA and PhD programmes, as well as faculty members. When demand is high, limited space and equipment may lead to crowding, waiting and difficulty planning a workout. I want to investigate whether better slot scheduling and occupancy information could make access more organized.
- Location: Gymkhana gym, NIT Jamshedpur, Jharkhand, India.
- Date first identified: 17 November 2026.
- People affected: Students and faculty who use the gym, and the gym guard/attendant who helps manage access. I will record the name and role of the person I speak with during fieldwork.
- Current situation: I have not yet confirmed how entry, attendance or busy periods are managed. During my visit, I will ask the guard/attendant about the current process and observe how users share the space and equipment. I will record only what I personally see or what the people I speak with tell me; I will not assume that the gym uses a paper register, fixed slots or any particular process.
2. Existing Solutions
Solution 1: Gymdesk — Online booking
- What it does: Lets members book sessions online, while gym administrators can set session capacity, booking rules and waitlists.
- Why it may not fit this situation: It is a general gym-management product. The NIT gym may need a smaller, campus-specific tool based on its actual capacity and college-approved access rules. I will compare these needs with the guard/attendant's feedback before deciding what is suitable.
Solution 2: MyGymDesk — Gym management platform
- What it does: Offers QR or biometric attendance, class schedules, slot booking, capacity management and member records, alongside billing and other commercial-gym features.
- Why it may not fit this situation: The campus gym's main requirement may be limited to managing crowd levels, time slots and attendance. A full commercial platform could include features that are unnecessary for this facility. Its suitability, cost and fit with college rules would need to be checked.

4. Initial Proposed Solution
I propose to build GymFlow, a simple campus gym crowd-management and slot-booking tool. Users could view scheduled slots and the reported occupancy level, then reserve an available slot within an approved capacity. The guard or another authorized person could record check-ins and check-outs so the displayed occupancy is based on entries and exits, not bookings alone. A colour indicator (green, yellow or red) could show low, moderate or near-capacity occupancy using thresholds agreed with the gym administration. Basic attendance history may also be included.

Separate sessions for students, faculty or other groups will be considered only if the gym administration confirms that they are needed and permitted. The first version will focus on slots, occupancy and attendance rather than personal fitness or medical records.
5. Questions to Validate During Fieldwork
1. What is the safe, practical capacity of the gym at one time?

4. Are there existing time slots or access rules for students and faculty?
5. Would slot booking and an occupancy display help? What would need to change?
6. Who could manage the system, and what attendance information may be recorded?
